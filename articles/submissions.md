## 1. O'Reilly Radar

**To:** workwithus@oreilly.com
**Subject:** Pitch: Why Agentic AI Demands Civil Engineering for Software

---

Dear O'Reilly Radar editors,

I'm writing to pitch an article for Radar on a thesis I believe complements your recent editorial direction: that agentic AI is the forcing function that makes the professionalization of software production economically inevitable, not merely desirable.

The argument draws a structural parallel with civil engineering's 19th-century transformation from craft to discipline. The difference between 10% productivity improvement and 10x is not better AI; it is better engineering: formal specification, verification, and accountability. The article identifies six foundational pillars that civil engineering rests on (formal specification, material standards, codes, simulation, licensure, education), maps them to software's current gaps, and proposes a concrete path forward: Event Modeling as the specification language, the Decider pattern as the executable bridge from specification to verifiable implementation, and a Terraform-analogous lifecycle (Specify, Plan, Verify, Apply, Observe) extended from infrastructure to products.

This builds directly on themes Radar has been exploring:

- Juliette van der Laarse's "Capability Architecture for AI-Native Engineering" (the observation that AI punishes the absence of engineering discipline)
- Mike Loukides's "Beyond Code Review" (specification-driven development as the emerging paradigm)
- Wes McKinney's "The Mythical Agent-Month" (engineering discipline for AI-assisted work)
- "Software Craftsmanship in the Age of AI" (the craft-to-discipline tension)

The full article (~6,000 words, 30 references, multiple diagrams) is published at:
https://martinrl.github.io/chronograph/software-civil-engineering

I'd be happy to adapt this for Radar in whichever format works best for you:

**(a) A condensed 1,500 to 2,000-word essay** focusing on the forcing function argument: why the transition is economically inevitable, what the infrastructure precedent (Terraform) teaches us, and what the specification gap looks like.

**(b) A 2 to 3-part installment series** that preserves the full depth:
  - Part 1: The problem and the civil engineering parallel (Sections 1-2)
  - Part 2: Event Modeling, the Decider pattern, and the specification lifecycle (Sections 3-4)
  - Part 3: Verification, organizational implications, and open gaps (Sections 6-10)

I'm the VP of Product & Engineering at ChronosHub, where we work with scholarly publishers (including IEEE) on publication workflow infrastructure. The article's thesis emerged from direct experience with specification-driven product development in a domain where auditability and formal process are non-negotiable.

I look forward to hearing your thoughts.

Best regards,
Martin Rosen-Lidholm
VP of Product & Engineering, ChronosHub
martin.rosenlidholm@gmail.com
