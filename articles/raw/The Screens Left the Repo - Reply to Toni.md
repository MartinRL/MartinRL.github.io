# Reply to Toni, round two (draft, 2026-09-07)

Toni, round two is as good as round one. Thank you. Point by point, and then two things I owe you.

1. **Intermediate models.** I take the devil's advocate seriously, and it sharpened the article. You are right that MDA's intermediates rotted because they were hand-touched, and that a generated, auto-validated intermediate would not rot the same way. But grant that, and the line moves rather than disappears: the intermediate exists in the workbench too. The materializer's output, a surface name plus a field bag, is your PIM for one screen. It is computed per request, held in memory, and gone. That is the difference between an intermediate and an artifact. A persisted intermediate, however well validated, can only detect drift after the fact; one that is never materialized makes drift unrepresentable. Traceability, which is the honest argument for persisting, I would rather serve by computing the intermediate on demand, Rewilding-style, than by storing it. And I hold the line on inferential transformations between models: an LLM there is my rung 2 relocated one level up. The LLM belongs above the top model, writing specs and residue against oracles, never between models. The draft now says all of this in the MDA paragraph.

2. **Regenerating the residue.** Your provocative question deserves a straight answer, and the answer is no, for a reason that is not about trust in weak models. Version control is for decisions, and the materializers are the decisions. The characterization suite pins exactly what it asserts; everything it leaves open would vary per regeneration. Regenerating would also put a stochastic transformer back on the build path, which is what the inner ring exists to end. So the strata part company at the repo boundary by their contract: equality leaves git, conformance stays. Where I agree with you completely is on *who* writes the residue: the context is one slice, one surface, a handful of oracles, and a small model does it fast and well. Written once, then versioned. The rocket article (https://chronograph.rosenlidholm.se/two-stage-sdd-rocket) makes the same argument for the domain residue.

3. **Parsers and linters at scale.** Conceded, and this is the live risk. xmlang's parser and linter are hand-written C#, a small number of rules over a small number of element kinds, and at product-suite scale that is rules times kinds maintained by hand. I have added it to "Where I might be wrong", together with the two ways the shared transformer stops being 369 lines: over-generalisation into exactly the mega-model you warned about, and abstraction leakage. Declarative rule definitions evaluated by a generic engine are the likely next step for the linter, and I would rather take it before the count forces it.

4. **Tokens as PIM, containers as PSM.** Yes, and that is precisely the cut "judgment as data, never geometry" makes: tokens and tiers are in the model, containers and breakpoints are the interpreter's business.
